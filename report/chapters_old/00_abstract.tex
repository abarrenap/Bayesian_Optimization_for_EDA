\begin{abstract}
This work evaluates Bayesian Optimization (BO) as a local-search operator within a continuous Estimation of Distribution Algorithm (EDA). A univariate Gaussian EDA is compared with two hybrids using L-BFGS-B and Gaussian-process BO with Expected Improvement. All methods are evaluated on Sphere, Rastrigin, Ackley, and Rosenbrock in five dimensions, using ten seeds and exactly 600 objective evaluations per run. Local refinement is restricted to the best population member and receives at most ten evaluations per generation. The plain EDA achieves the lowest median final objective on all four benchmarks. BO improves on the median performance of the L-BFGS-B hybrid on three benchmarks, but does not compensate consistently for the population evaluations it replaces. These results show that adding surrogate-guided refinement is not automatically beneficial under a small shared evaluation budget. The conclusions concern this specific short-budget implementation; they do not establish a general ranking of local optimizers.
\keywords{Estimation of Distribution Algorithms \and Bayesian Optimization \and Local search \and Evaluation budget}
\end{abstract}
